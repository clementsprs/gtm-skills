# Compliance and ethics for generated video

Not legal advice. These are the lines that kept real ads approved and out of trouble. Check
your own jurisdiction and platform policies.

## Generated people

- **A synthetic person saying "I use [product]" or giving personal results is a fake
  testimonial** (US FTC rule on consumer reviews and testimonials, 16 CFR Part 465; EU unfair
  commercial practices rules; national consumer law). Two clean options:
  1. **Host rewrite (preferred):** the person is a presenter talking to the viewer ("you
     can..."), never claiming to be a customer, never first-person results.
  2. **Label it:** "AI-generated actor · dramatization", small but legible, on screen while
     they speak.
- **EU AI Act transparency (Art. 50):** realistic synthetic people or scenes shown to EU
  audiences should be disclosed as AI-generated. Plan a label for EU placements even with a
  host script, and check the current guidance.
- Platforms (Meta, YouTube, TikTok, LinkedIn) have their own AI disclosure toggles or labels
  for realistic synthetic media. Use them.
- **Never put a real person's name, title or quote over a generated face.** Move a real
  customer's quote to narration or a text card with attribution.
- **Never generate a real, identifiable person** (celebrities, public figures, employees,
  customers) without their written consent. Don't prompt "looks like [famous person]".
- Generated scenes in a customer story carry "Illustrative scenes" on the end card.

## Data and names on screen

- Every company, person, deal and number on screen is fictional, or a real published figure
  with the source on screen.
- Check invented company names against real companies before shipping (a made-up name that
  turns out to be real is a takedown). Avoid AI-cliché names (Acme, Northwind, Meridian).
- Never let real customer or prospect data leak from a product demo environment into a frame.
- Every product claim maps to something live and public: no roadmap, no unpublished metrics,
  no implied capabilities.

## Logos and marks

- Your own logo: always the official file, composited, never generated.
- Customer logos in paid media: written OK from the customer first.
- Third-party marks: nominative use only (to say you integrate with X), redrawn or official
  per their brand guidelines, never generated.
- Watch for stray real brands the model invents on cups, laptops, clothes: edit them out.

## Comparative and regional rules

- Naming competitors in ads is restricted in some markets (e.g. France: comparative advertising
  has strict conditions). When unsure, "your old tool" beats a name.
- Respect local language and address norms in on-screen copy.

## Music and voices

- Use music you generated or licensed for the placement. Don't prompt "in the style of [named
  artist]".
- Cloned voices only from people who consented (your own actors, or the generated character's
  own take for consistency).
